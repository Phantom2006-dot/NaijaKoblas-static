# Staff portal and moderation workflow audit

**Scope:** staff routes, APIs, seller oversight, product intake, notifications, suspension/reinstatement behavior, and separation of staff duties from administrator duties. **Audit date:** 2026-11-02. **Project:** `/home/ubuntu/workspace/NaijaKoblas-static`.

## Executive summary

The implementation has a credible authorization foundation: the authoritative database role is loaded from the authenticated Clerk identity, staff procedures require the `seller:review` capability, administrator procedures require the `admin` role, seller-owned product rows remain owned by `sellerUserId`, and staff-created listings are deliberately drafts that the seller must review and publish. Seller suspension is transactional, records an audit event, notifies the seller, disables the store, and leaves a narrow paid-order fulfilment path available.

The staff workspace is nevertheless incomplete as a moderation system. `/staff` currently combines legacy seller-application decisions with a product-intake form, but has no product review queue, listing moderation state, moderation decision record, evidence/history view, or staff-facing operational notification inbox. The staff API can create drafts and upload images, but cannot review or disposition existing products. The admin page embeds the same staff intake component, while staff cannot access the genuinely administrative functions (role management, payment reconciliation); this is technically protected but presents an unclear workspace boundary. Seller self-activation also auto-approves pending legacy applications, making the staff approval UI a compatibility/recovery tool rather than a consistently required gate. Reinstatement exists as a status transition, but there is no dedicated reason, case, escalation, or structured reinstatement workflow.

## Findings

1. **High — There is no product moderation workflow, only seller review and staff-assisted draft creation.** The schema has product statuses `draft | published | archived` but no `pending_review`, `rejected`, moderation reason, reviewer, reviewed-at, or version/evidence fields ([`drizzle/schema.ts:17-20`](drizzle/schema.ts#L17-L20), [`drizzle/schema.ts:137-156`](drizzle/schema.ts#L137-L156)). The staff router exposes only `activeSellers`, `createProductForSeller`, `sellerApplications`, and `reviewSellerApplication` ([`server/routers.ts:291-325`](server/routers.ts#L291-L325)). There is no staff query for all listings, no moderation decision mutation, and no queue filtered by listing risk/status. **Impact:** staff cannot systematically inspect, approve, reject, archive, or request changes on seller-created catalog content. Product intake is operational assistance, not moderation.

2. **High — The staff route is an incomplete workspace and does not present a coherent queue-based workflow.** `/staff` renders `StaffDashboardPage` followed by `StaffProductIntakePanel` ([`client/src/App.tsx:31-35`](client/src/App.tsx#L31-L35), [`client/src/pages/StaffWorkspacePage.tsx:1-6`](client/src/pages/StaffWorkspacePage.tsx#L1-L6)). The dashboard loads every seller application with no status filter, search, pagination, assignment, priority, or detail view and offers only Approve/Decline/Suspend/Re-approve buttons ([`client/src/pages/AccountPages.tsx:165-171`](client/src/pages/AccountPages.tsx#L165-L171)). **Impact:** staff cannot triage a growing queue, see a seller’s listings/orders/history in context, distinguish newly submitted work from already resolved records, or hand off cases safely.

3. **High — Seller self-service can bypass the apparent staff approval boundary for pending applications.** `account.becomeSeller` calls `activateSellerAccount` ([`server/routers.ts:96-105`](server/routers.ts#L96-L105]); when a pending application exists, the transaction changes it to `approved`, clears reviewer fields, and activates the seller account/store ([`server/db.ts:322-368`](server/db.ts#L322-L368)). The staff UI still describes pending records as resolvable by staff and offers approval/decline controls ([`client/src/pages/AccountPages.tsx:165-171`](client/src/pages/AccountPages.tsx#L165-L171)). **Impact:** the governance meaning of “pending” is ambiguous: staff may think approval is mandatory while a seller can approve the record through self-service. The code is not an authorization bypass for privileged data, but it undermines a predictable moderation policy and audit interpretation.

4. **Medium — Suspension/reinstatement is secure at the state-transition level but too coarse for a real moderation case.** Allowed transitions are `pending -> approved/rejected`, `approved -> suspended`, and `suspended -> approved` ([`server/_core/authorization.ts:19-24`](server/_core/authorization.ts#L19-L24)). The transition transaction changes the user role, toggles store activity, writes one generic audit event, and sends one seller notification ([`server/db.ts:459-535`](server/db.ts#L459-L535)). The staff UI supplies hard-coded notes such as “Seller access paused by the staff portal” and “Seller access restored by the staff portal” ([`client/src/pages/AccountPages.tsx:171-171`](client/src/pages/AccountPages.tsx#L171-L171)). **Impact:** there is no required suspension reason/category, evidence, effective date, appeal/reinstatement request, second-review requirement, or structured case history. A later reviewer cannot distinguish fraud, policy, operational, or temporary safety causes from the audit metadata alone.

5. **Medium — The staff product-intake flow correctly preserves seller publishing control, but its authority and provenance are underspecified.** `createProductForStaff` locks and verifies an approved seller with an active store, writes the product under the seller’s immutable owner ID, records the acting staff user, and sends the seller a draft notification ([`server/db.ts:681-723`](server/db.ts#L681-L723)). The UI explicitly says the seller must review and publish ([`client/src/pages/StaffProductIntakePanel.tsx:37-48`](client/src/pages/StaffProductIntakePanel.tsx#L37-L48)). This is a good moderation boundary. However, the intake form has no intake reason, source/reference, staff notes, seller acknowledgement, edit history, or “awaiting seller review” queue state. **Impact:** staff can prepare content, but neither side has a durable workflow record explaining why it was created or what remains to be reviewed.

6. **Medium — Staff can upload images for seller listings, but there is no staff review or correction lifecycle for media.** The upload transaction allows an authenticated seller owner, staff, or admin to add images, enforces seller approval/active-store and listing ownership, records a staff audit event, and notifies the seller ([`server/db.ts:760-837`](server/db.ts#L760-L837)). Primary-image selection is ownership-scoped but does not itself require active store or a staff capability ([`server/db.ts:839-863`](server/db.ts#L839-L863)). The staff intake UI exposes upload immediately after creating a draft ([`client/src/pages/StaffProductIntakePanel.tsx:47-47`](client/src/pages/StaffProductIntakePanel.tsx#L47-L47)). **Impact:** the initial upload boundary is strong, but staff cannot flag/remove/reorder/reject unsuitable media through a moderation workflow, and media changes do not transition a listing into a review state.

7. **Medium — Staff and administrator workspace composition communicates the wrong duty split.** The admin page renders `AdminDashboardPage` and then the entire `StaffProductIntakePanel` ([`client/src/pages/AdminAccessPage.tsx:59-59`](client/src/pages/AdminAccessPage.tsx#L59-L59)). Administrators inherit staff capabilities in the server capability map ([`server/_core/authorization.ts:12-17`](server/_core/authorization.ts#L12-L17)), which is reasonable for emergency coverage, but the UI does not label the embedded intake as delegated staff work or offer a clear “staff operations” boundary. Conversely, staff have no admin access to role management or payment reconciliation: those procedures are `adminProcedure` only ([`server/routers.ts:328-400`](server/routers.ts#L328-L400)). **Impact:** the separation is enforced technically but not expressed as an information architecture. Admins see staff tools mixed into an account/payment console, while staff lack a clear view of what they may escalate rather than execute.

8. **Medium — Notifications are private and transactional, but staff do not receive the operational events needed to run moderation.** Notifications are scoped by `recipientUserId` in reads and mark operations, capped at 50 items, and polled every 45 seconds ([`server/db.ts:1393-1429`](server/db.ts#L1393-L1429), [`client/src/components/store/NotificationBell.tsx:7-70`](client/src/components/store/NotificationBell.tsx#L7-L70)). Seller application submission does notify all current staff/admin users in the legacy application path ([`server/db.ts:235-265`](server/db.ts#L235-L265)), but self-service activation only notifies the seller and staff access changes notify the target account ([`server/db.ts:370-395`](server/db.ts#L370-L395), [`server/db.ts:539-576`](server/db.ts#L539-L576)). There are no staff notifications for a seller’s product submission, an intake draft awaiting seller review, a suspension appeal/reinstatement request, or a listing requiring moderation. **Impact:** the inbox is safe but cannot function as a moderation work queue; staff must poll the entire seller application list and have no alert on the most important future moderation events.

9. **Low — Staff route access is server-protected, but route-level loading/error states do not clearly distinguish staff from admin or preserve deep-link intent.** `/staff` is registered in the general storefront router, while `/admin` is rendered outside the storefront shell ([`client/src/App.tsx:16-40`](client/src/App.tsx#L16-L40)). The staff dashboard maps any query error to a generic “Staff access required” page ([`client/src/pages/AccountPages.tsx:165-170`](client/src/pages/AccountPages.tsx#L165-L170)); there is no explicit role-aware sign-in/return flow comparable to the admin gate. **Impact:** unauthorized users are denied safely, but staff onboarding and troubleshooting are less clear, and a non-staff customer cannot tell whether the issue is missing access, expired session, or temporary API/database failure.

10. **Low — Audit history is append-only but not queryable in either operational workspace.** Privileged changes write `accountAuditEvents` for seller review, staff access, product drafts, and staff image uploads ([`drizzle/schema.ts:80-91`](drizzle/schema.ts#L80-L91), [`server/db.ts:515-520`](server/db.ts#L515-L524), [`server/db.ts:708-712`](server/db.ts#L708-L713), [`server/db.ts:819-825`](server/db.ts#L819-L825)). There is no staff/admin API or UI to retrieve a seller/product case timeline. **Impact:** audit data exists for forensic use but not for daily moderation decisions, handoffs, appeals, or quality control.

## Recommendations

1. **Define the policy boundary first.** Choose either (a) seller self-activation is the product policy, in which case retire the notion of pending approval for that path and label the staff view “legacy applications and safety actions,” or (b) staff approval is mandatory, in which case `becomeSeller` must not convert `pending` to `approved`. Keep a separate migration/compatibility path for legacy records and make audit events explicit about self-activation versus human review.

2. **Build `/staff` as a role-specific operations workspace with four explicit queues:** seller applications/legacy records, listing intake awaiting seller review, product moderation/review, and suspended/reinstatement cases. Add filters for status, age, priority, seller, assigned staff member, and last activity; use cursor pagination; and provide a seller/product detail drawer with history and safe links.

3. **Add a product moderation model rather than overloading seller product status.** Introduce a separate moderation status such as `not_submitted | pending_review | changes_requested | approved | rejected | blocked`, reviewer/decision timestamps, structured reason codes, and immutable decision notes. Keep `published` as the seller-controlled commercial publication state. Staff can approve/block/request changes, but cannot publish a seller listing unless a separately documented emergency policy exists.

4. **Formalize staff intake provenance.** Add intake source/reason, staff notes, optional campaign/reference ID, and a durable “seller review requested” event to the product workflow. Keep `sellerUserId` immutable, default all intake to draft, notify the seller, and require seller acknowledgement or edit before publication. Display who created/edited each field and when.

5. **Make suspension a case workflow.** Require a structured reason, evidence/attachment references if supported, scope (store/listings/account), effective timestamp, reviewer note, and optional expiry. Add an appeal/reinstatement request procedure that creates a staff queue item; retain paid-order fulfilment as a narrowly scoped capability; require a clear reinstatement decision and notify the seller with the permitted capabilities. Preserve every transition in a case timeline.

6. **Add operational staff notifications without leaking data.** Notify the staff role group or an assigned queue for new moderation submissions, seller appeals, suspicious listing changes, and overdue cases. Continue recipient-scoped inbox reads and same-origin links. Make event keys stable per case/state transition, and add unread queue counts to the staff workspace rather than relying only on a generic bell.

7. **Separate `/staff` and `/admin` information architecture.** Staff should see seller safety, listing moderation, intake, cases, and operational notifications. Admin should see staff access lifecycle, payment reconciliation, system configuration, and audit/reporting. Admin may have an explicit “Open staff operations” link for coverage, but do not silently append staff panels to the admin dashboard. Keep all server-side procedure gates as the authoritative boundary.

8. **Expose audit timelines where decisions happen.** Add staff/admin read-only procedures for seller, product, and case timelines, with actor, timestamp, old/new state, reason, and linked notification. Do not expose unrelated customer contact/payment data to staff; reveal order/customer details only in a separately authorized operational context.

9. **Improve staff route behavior and testing.** Add an explicit staff gate that distinguishes unauthenticated, wrong-role, and service-error states; preserve `returnTo=/staff`; and add integration tests for customer/staff/admin access, product moderation ownership, intake draft invariants, notification recipients, suspension-to-fulfilment-only behavior, reinstatement, and concurrency conflicts.

10. **Keep the existing good boundaries.** Retain server-side Clerk/database role authority, `staffProcedure`/`adminProcedure`, immutable seller ownership, active-store checks for intake and media upload, transactional audit + seller notification writes, same-origin notification links, and the rule that staff-created listings remain private until the seller reviews and publishes them ([`server/_core/context.ts:18-52`](server/_core/context.ts#L18-L52), [`server/_core/trpc.ts:42-50`](server/_core/trpc.ts#L42-L50), [`server/db.ts:681-723`](server/db.ts#L681-L723)).
